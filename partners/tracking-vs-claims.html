GlucoBeacon · plan & employer economics

A member who tracks costs a plan less than a member who doesn’t.

This is a transparent model of that gap, run twice: once for diabetes (11% of U.S. adults) and once for hypertension (47.7% — over 4× the population, and a condition GlucoBeacon already tracks). A third section covers a different kind of return: protecting the GLP-1 spend a plan is already paying for, whether or not it works out. Every input below is editable and every assumption is sourced.

The intuition first

One avoided claim pays for hundreds of members.

GlucoBeacon Pro sells to consumers for $79.99 once. To a plan, at a bulk per-activation rate of roughly $30 per member for a lifetime entitlement, a single averted diabetes event covers a large block of licenses. The model — diabetes

Tracker vs. non-tracker, across a book of business.

The mechanism is not treatment. It is better data → better clinical decisions, better day-to-day choices, and visible medication adherence. Published trials of comparable apps put the effect at a 0.2–0.6 percentage-point drop in A1C for engaged users (up to 1.1 pp when a clinician reviews the data). U.S. claims analyses value each 1.0 pp of A1C reduction at roughly $430–$740 in avoided annual cost. Adjust anything below.

The model — hypertension

The same mechanism, a much bigger population.

47.7% of U.S. adults have hypertension6 — over 4× diabetes prevalence, and BP tracking is a feature GlucoBeacon already ships, not a build. The catch, and it matters: self-monitoring alone barely moves blood pressure. The strongest meta-analysis found no significant effect from monitoring by itself (−1.0 mmHg), a pooled effect of roughly 3.3–3.8 mmHg systolic across self-monitoring trials generally, and up to 6.1 mmHg when monitoring is paired with real behavior support — education, lifestyle coaching, medication follow-up.7 Self-monitored BP programs have been associated with roughly $7,794 in avoided cardiovascular cost per person over 20 years (≈$390/yr).8 Adjust anything below.

A third angle — not a calculator

Protecting GLP-1 spend you’re already committed to.

GLP-1s are the fastest-growing line in employer pharmacy spend — up from 6.9% of pharmacy spend in 2023 to 10.5% in 2025, and over 15% of total annual claims for more than a quarter of employers.9 That spend happens whether or not it works out: up to 70% of users discontinue within a year in real-world cohorts (range 37–81% depending on setting), and up to 39% of the weight lost can be lean muscle mass when nutrition support is missing — which itself drives the weight back on if the drug is later stopped.10 Only about half of GLP-1 patients report adequate dietary guidance, and barely a third report adequate activity support.

This isn’t a cost-avoidance case like the two models above — it’s a spend-protection case. GlucoBeacon already ships weight logging, medication logging (drug-name-agnostic — it doesn’t care whether the entry is metformin or Zepbound), food tracking, and activity tracking. Layering that onto an existing GLP-1 benefit gives members the adherence and nutrition support tied to durable outcomes, on spend the plan is paying for regardless. No new calculator here on purpose — there isn’t yet a published, sourced dollar-per-member figure for this specific mechanism the way there is for A1C or blood pressure, and inventing one would be exactly the kind of unsupported number this whole page is trying to avoid.

How billing works

The plan is billed only for members who opt in.

GlucoBeacon appears as a tile or single-sign-on link inside the plan’s existing member hub — in the digital-health or diabetes-care section, co-branded if the plan wants. Nothing is charged for eligible-but-inactive members.

Activation event
A member taps the hub link. A signed deep link carries a plan token, the app installs, and a lifetime Pro entitlement is provisioned to that member. That tap is the billable unit — unambiguous and auditable.
What the plan pays
A one-time fee per activated member for a lifetime entitlement, or a lower per-member annual fee. Volume tiers. No PMPM on the whole diabetic population.
Reporting
Monthly activation count and running spend against any cap the plan sets. Aggregate engagement (tracker rate, mean logging frequency) — never individual member data.
Data
Member logs stay on the member’s device by default. GlucoBeacon does not send claims-linkable health data to the plan, and the plan’s payment does not buy access to it. A pilot’s outcome analysis runs on the plan’s own claims, matched to a control group.

Read this before you model with it

What would make these numbers wrong.

  1. No GlucoBeacon-specific outcomes study exists yet. Effect sizes are borrowed from meta-analyses of comparable apps. A claims-matched pilot with a control group is the only way to get a real number for this product.
  2. The A1C effect is small and range-bound. Without an integrated clinician-feedback loop — which GlucoBeacon does not have today — expect the low end (−0.2 to −0.4 pp), not the −1.1 pp seen in clinician-supervised trials.
  3. Engagement is the whole ballgame. If the sustained-tracker rate is 15% instead of 40%, the return falls by roughly two-thirds. This is the input to pressure-test hardest.
  4. Plan churn caps the upside. Members change plans every ~3 years on average, so the plan that pays for the app captures only the near-term claims reduction, not the decade of avoided complications.
  5. Opt-in members are not average. People who tap the link are more health-engaged and might have improved anyway. A valid pilot needs a matched, non-enrolled control cohort.
  6. Vendor ROI runs hot. Published returns from larger digital-diabetes programs tend to exceed independent replication. This model deliberately uses independent figures and stays at the conservative end.
  7. Hypertension's return depends entirely on real support, not logging. The strongest meta-analysis found self-monitoring alone produces no significant BP change — the −3.5 mmHg base case and $390/yr figure already assume monitoring is paired with something beyond a bare log screen. Verify that assumption in a pilot before scaling it.
  8. This is not a medical claim. GlucoBeacon does not diagnose or treat. The pathway to lower cost is information and behavior, and it only works for members who use it.

Ways to structure it

Roughly easiest to hardest.

Member-hub, per-activation
The model above. Plan embeds the link, pays per opt-in, lifetime entitlement. Lowest friction, fastest to pilot.
Medicare Advantage supplemental benefit
Position as a health-related supplemental benefit; MA plans compete on benefit richness and Star ratings. Per-activation or flat annual.
Digital-health formulary listing
Get vetted onto a PBM digital formulary or a navigator (Rightway, Personify). Requires SOC 2 / HITRUST and this economic dossier; opens many plans at once.
Value-based / shared savings
Lower base fee plus a bonus tied to measured A1C improvement or reduced admissions in the enrolled cohort. Higher trust bar, needs a claims-data partnership.
Self-insured employers
Often faster than carriers. Sold PEPM through benefits consultants (Mercer, Aon, WTW) or a PEO channel.
Medicaid MCOs & FQHCs
High diabetes burden, cost-sensitive, value-based mandates in many states. Full Spanish / Filipino / Hindi localization is a real differentiator here.
HEDIS / Star angle
Comprehensive Diabetes Care measures (A1C testing and control) feed MA Star ratings, which feed plan revenue — a growth argument, not just a cost one.
